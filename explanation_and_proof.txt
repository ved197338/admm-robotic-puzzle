=========================================================
CONSENSUS ADMM: A CLEAR AND ACCESSIBLE EXPLANATION
=========================================================

1. THE PROBLEM
We have a simulated grid puzzle that is assembled by two independent agents: Robotic Arm 1 and Robotic Arm 2. 
- Arm 1 controls the left half of the puzzle (Region 1).
- Arm 2 controls the right half of the puzzle (Region 2).
- There is a strip of puzzle right in the middle where both regions overlap (Region 12).

Initially, both arms have different "local preferences" (different matrix values) for what the overlap region should look like. If they just output their preferences independently, the puzzle will be broken and mismatched at the boundary. 

Our goal is to run a mathematical optimization algorithm called Consensus ADMM. This algorithm forces the two arms to negotiate and agree on the exact same values for the overlap region, while minimizing any unnecessary changes to the rest of the puzzle.

---------------------------------------------------------
2. CODE BREAKDOWN (How the ADMM Algorithm works)
---------------------------------------------------------

*** config.py & puzzle_env.py (The Setup) ***
These files build the matrices. `puzzle_env.py` creates a smooth wave pattern (the ideal global puzzle) and then purposefully adds random noise and shifts the values at the overlap boundary to simulate a severe disagreement between Arm 1 and Arm 2. 

*** arm_models.py (The Local Update) ***
Each Robotic Arm wants to stick to its initial preference. We define a "Local Cost" function, which mathematically penalizes the arm the further its final matrix deviates from its original preference.
- The ADMM 'x-update' (Local Update): During each step of the negotiation, each arm looks at the proposed middle-ground (called the Consensus Variable 'z'). The arm then computes the best compromise: it updates its own local puzzle values (the 'x' matrix) to move closer to the consensus 'z', but tries to keep its Local Cost penalty as low as possible. Because this problem is quadratic, the code uses a fast algebraic shortcut to instantly find this balance.

*** consensus_admm.py (The Global Coordinator) ***
This file acts as the central coordinator that drives the arms to a final agreement over a repeated loop.
- The 'z-update' (Consensus Variable): The coordinator asks both arms for their current boundary values, and simply averages them to propose a new, fair middle-ground 'z'.
- The 'u-update' (Dual Variables): This is a mathematical penalty for disagreement. If an arm is stubbornly refusing to align its boundary with the consensus 'z', the coordinator increases the Dual Variable 'u'. This 'u' acts as mounting numerical pressure that forces the arm to yield on the next iteration.

*** ml_model.py (The Machine Learning Accelerator) ***
Normally, the coordinator starts its first guess for the consensus variable 'z' at exactly zero, which takes many iterations to fix. Here, we trained a lightweight Machine Learning model (Ridge Regression) on past puzzle disagreements. It looks at the initial gap between Arm 1 and Arm 2 and instantly predicts what the final consensus 'z' should be. By seeding ADMM with this smart prediction, the algorithm converges much faster.

---------------------------------------------------------
3. HOW TO PROVE THE ALGORITHM IS CORRECT
---------------------------------------------------------
To mathematically prove that Consensus ADMM actually solved the puzzle perfectly, you track three key metrics (which are logged in the terminal and plotted as graphs).

PROOF 1: The Primal Residual goes to Zero
- What it is: The numerical difference between Arm 1's boundary matrix and Arm 2's boundary matrix. 
- Why it proves success: When this value drops to exactly 0 (or below our tolerance of 1e-4), it is mathematically guaranteed that Arm 1 and Arm 2 are producing the exact same values on the overlap. The boundary gap is closed, meaning they have reached perfect consensus.

PROOF 2: The Dual Residual goes to Zero
- What it is: The amount the consensus variable 'z' changes from one iteration to the next.
- Why it proves success: When the Dual Residual reaches 0, the coordinator no longer needs to update the middle-ground. The system has reached a stable mathematical equilibrium where the arms have stopped negotiating and are fully settled.

PROOF 3: The Objective Value Converges
- What it is: The sum of the Local Costs (penalties) for both arms.
- Why it proves success: In the plot `objective.png`, the cost spikes initially as the arms are forced to compromise, but then flattens out perfectly into a horizontal line. This proves the algorithm didn't just find any random agreement—it found the absolute mathematically optimal agreement that minimizes the overall deviation from their original plans.

PROOF 4: Visual Verification
Look at the generated image `results/plots/final_puzzle.png`. Because there is no jagged line, abrupt color shift, or numerical clash in the center strip, it visually confirms that the two disconnected matrices were flawlessly stitched into a single, consistent global puzzle state.
